# Wasl MVP production: owner action and technical handoff guide

**Snapshot:** 2026-10-26. **Status:** planning/handoff, not evidence of launch readiness. Source of truth for scope and task order: [IMPLEMENTATION_GUIDE.md](IMPLEMENTATION_GUIDE.md), [PROJECT_HANDOFF.md](PROJECT_HANDOFF.md), and [phase-7.md](phase-7.md). This file is not legal advice. Provider screens and prices can change; follow the linked provider documentation if a label differs. Do not enter secrets, personal data, legal drafts, backup files, or MFA recovery codes into this tracked Markdown file or a public issue/chat.

## Read this first: who does what

- **We can do now, without another owner login or secret:** implement and test local/mock technical hardening; prepare E2E fixtures, authorization and abuse matrices, accessibility/RTL and indexing audits, load/performance measurement scripts, provider-outage simulations, recovery runbooks, and staging/CI configuration _drafts_. We can inspect the existing development environment, but must verify the target before any database change. These preparations are **not** substitutes for live provider and deployment checks.
- **You must do or explicitly authorize:** own/pay for accounts, domains and infrastructure; approve budgets and environments; securely provision provider keys; choose release/performance baselines; create real test/admin identities and enroll your own MFA; obtain qualified legal/privacy decisions and approve final copy; select a release and sign off on staging/production. Never let an agent guess a legal age limit, consent basis, or data-retention period.
- **After you hand these off, we do:** connect and verify integrations, implement approved Phase 7 privacy flows, test staging/production, configure controlled CI/CD, investigate failures, and write evidence. Do not claim legal certification, guaranteed provider delivery, or a production-ready product on the basis of mock tests.

## Your exact action sequence and what to send back

Use the checklist in order; items that require a provider account may run in parallel. For every completed item, send **only nonsecret identifiers/status/decisions** in chat. Put keys and passwords into an ignored local `server/.env`, a secure secret manager, or the intended service's encrypted environment settings; tell us _where they are available_, not their values. Keep a password manager, MFA and billing ownership under your control. If something is already configured, verify and report it rather than creating duplicates.

### 1. Choose project ownership, domain and release boundaries

1. Decide the legal operator/data controller, primary technical owner, backup operator, support/privacy contact, and who can approve production changes. Choose a support mailbox and security/privacy request intake mailbox you actually monitor; decide who responds and when.
2. Register or confirm ownership of the public domain at a registrar/DNS host. Enable account MFA, auto-renewal, and a backup owner. Choose canonical **client**, **API**, **staging client/API**, and **email-sending subdomain** hostnames (e.g. `app.example.com`, `api.example.com`, `staging.example.com`, `staging-api.example.com`, `mail.example.com`; these are examples, not assigned Wasl URLs). Confirm you can edit DNS. Do not put credentials or private DNS tokens into Git.
3. Decide if production may use Render-provided domains first or must wait for the custom domain. Record your planned audience/region, launch date, expected peak active users, billing owner, monthly budget/alerts, release window and rollback approver.
4. Provide us: actual intended hostnames, domain owner/DNS provider name, support/privacy contact addresses, budget and release approver, and whether staging must be inaccessible to the public. **Do not** hand over registrar login. We will translate the decisions into environment variables, CORS, redirects, SEO and routing checks.

### 2. Get a qualified privacy/legal decision package — Phase 7 release blocker

1. Engage a qualified adviser for the intended Türkiye launch. Give them [IMPLEMENTATION_GUIDE.md](IMPLEMENTATION_GUIDE.md) sections 1–4 and describe the actual data flows: Supabase Auth/Postgres/Storage, Stream chat, Resend email, Render hosting/logs, user-generated content/reports, participation/history, users in Türkiye and any EU reach. Ask for decisions, not a generic template.
2. Ask for the identified data controller/contact, legally approved **versioned Arabic and English** privacy notice, terms/UGC/reporting notice, cookie/analytics notice **if actually applicable**, and any affirmative acceptance or optional consent wording. Specify which checkbox(es) are required, optional, independent, and what event/version/time/identity must be recorded. Ask for translations to be reviewed, not machine text accepted without review.
3. Obtain a written minimum-age/age-assurance/guardian policy; do not pick a number from an assumption. Specify what the app does for a user who does not qualify and whether existing users need to reaccept new versions.
4. Obtain a data inventory/retention and deletion decision **per class**: account and profile, uploaded files, community/event records and participation history, discussion posts, Stream messages/DMs, reports/moderation evidence, notifications/email delivery logs, audit/security logs and backup copies. Specify lawful basis, restricted-access exception, erasure versus anonymization, export format, response/verification deadline, provider deletion steps, backup expiry and legal holds. Ask counsel to explicitly resolve Türkiye KVKK/5651/copyright/UGC obligations, transfers and conditional GDPR/EU applicability; get processor/DPA and cross-border transfer decisions for each vendor.
5. Specify identity verification and abuse controls for access/export/deletion requests, who processes them, appeals/complaints and how to handle a request while an investigation or legal hold exists. Approve a version/date/signatory and keep the signed source in a controlled location.
6. Provide us: a decision table and approved publication-ready texts/version IDs, plus the authorized reviewer/sign-off status and owner of privacy requests. Use a secure, access-controlled channel for unpublished legal drafts; send paths/links with appropriate permissions, not personal data in the repository. We can then implement Tasks 147–148 and record Task 149 review; **we cannot sign for counsel or for you**.

### 3. Prepare Supabase environments and Auth settings

**Existing state:** the connected Supabase project is **development**, not an approved production target; development migrations through `0020` are reported applied. Stream development credentials have been verified historically. Do not reuse the development database as production or point a new staging environment at production.

1. In the [Supabase dashboard](https://supabase.com/dashboard), keep the existing development project. Create **separate staging and production projects** in the organization/account you own; choose regions deliberately with your privacy adviser and record project refs/regions/plan, but do not post passwords. Select a plan/backups policy that meets your approved recovery objective. Read the [backup policy](https://supabase.com/docs/guides/platform/backups.md): scheduled database backup availability varies by plan; database backup **does not include Storage object contents**. Arrange independent object retention/backup as needed. Never test restore against the live production project.
2. In each project, locate Project URL and publishable API key in the dashboard's API keys area; these are client configuration. Locate a server-only secret/service-role key only if required by the code and store it in that environment's server secret settings. Obtain a database connection string for the correct project from the Connect/Database UI and store it only in server secrets. Record which credential goes with dev, staging and production. Do not put privileged keys or `DATABASE_URL` in `client/`, Vite variables or Markdown.
3. Under Auth settings, check email/password confirmation, allowed sign-in methods, site URL and **exact redirect URLs** for each environment (including recovery/invite callback behavior). Decide whether account creation is public at launch. Set strong password policy and enable [leaked-password protection](https://supabase.com/docs/guides/auth/password-security.md) if the chosen plan supports it; Phase 3 recorded it disabled. Note whether the plan requires an upgrade rather than claiming it is enabled. Configure production Auth email sender/custom SMTP as needed for real verification/recovery mail; **Resend app notification API setup alone does not automatically configure Supabase Auth emails**. Verify confirmation and reset to a mailbox you control.
4. Create separate test identities in **staging**, using nonpersonal fixture content: normal user, community/event organizer, organization owner/admin, moderator, waitlisted/accepted participant, blocked/suspended user, and an intended platform admin. Keep credentials in a password manager or an access-controlled test secret store, not chat or committed Playwright fixtures. Record actual Wasl `identity.users.id` for the admin **after signing up and mapping into Wasl**, not the Supabase `auth.users.id`: `PLATFORM_ADMIN_USER_IDS` expects Wasl IDs. Add the admin ID only to the appropriate server environment.
5. Use an authenticator app on the admin account to enroll TOTP and verify the challenge to reach `aal2` using Wasl `/admin`; keep factor/recovery details yourself. [Supabase MFA documentation](https://supabase.com/docs/guides/auth/auth-mfa/totp.md) explains enrollment and verification. Ask us to verify both allowed AAL2 and denied AAL1 paths without sharing your one-time code.
6. Provide us: staging/prod project refs, regions, plan/backup status, allowed Auth origins, public project URLs/publishable keys or their **secure configuration location**, confirmation that server-only keys/connection strings are installed in the right environment, admin Wasl ID and whether AAL2 sign-in succeeds. Do not disclose passwords or codes. Authorize staging migrations explicitly after we identify and double-check its target; approve production migration separately after backup/rehearsal.

### 4. Configure Resend for real transactional notifications

1. Create or confirm a [Resend](https://resend.com/) organization/account you own; enable MFA, billing and usage limits/alerts. Check sending limits for expected MVP volume. Choose distinct API keys for staging and production; use least-privilege **sending** permission and domain restriction if available, not a broad shared key.
2. Choose an email sending subdomain you own (for example `mail.example.com`; no assumption that you already own it). Follow [Resend: add and verify a domain](https://resend.com/docs/add-a-domain.md): Dashboard → Domains → Add Domain; select region, then copy **exact** provider-issued DNS DKIM and SPF entries into your DNS host, avoid proxying mail verification CNAMEs, wait for verification and add a DMARC policy agreed with your mail administrator. Do not invent SPF/DKIM values or replace existing MX/SPF blindly. Decide whether to disable open/click tracking for privacy-sensitive transactional mail.
3. Choose a verified from-address, e.g. `notifications@mail.example.com`, and a monitored reply/support address. Create a [Resend API key](https://resend.com/docs/dashboard/api-keys/introduction) for each environment and copy its value **once** into the secure server environment as `RESEND_API_KEY`; set `RESEND_FROM_EMAIL` to the exact sender address. Never put either key in the browser, tracked `.env.example`, GitHub issue or this file. The server sends through `server/src/modules/notifications/resend-email.adapter.ts`; a worker in `notification-email.worker.ts` only starts when the required variables and database are present. Ensure the deployed API stays running or agree on a dedicated worker topology before accepting delivery reliability.
4. Give us a staging recipient address you control and permission to send **a small number of test messages**, plus the verified domain/sender, whether staging key and production key have been installed, any quota/restrictions and a screenshot/status of domain verification with key values hidden. We will test create → queue → provider acceptance → recipient delivery/failure handling, retries, idempotency and log/worker behavior. Production delivery is a separate go-live check.

### 5. Review Stream Chat deployment and real test identities

1. In [Stream's dashboard](https://getstream.io/dashboard/), keep the already verified **development** app separate. Decide whether staging and production get separate Stream apps (recommended isolation); create them under your owned account, select region/plan and store API key plus server-only API secret in the appropriate environments. Never expose `STREAM_API_SECRET` through `VITE_` values.
2. In **each** environment, verify the `messaging` channel type/roles: nonmembers cannot read or join protected channels or create resource channels; members can read/send; `channel_moderator` has only scoped moderator powers. Set `STREAM_PERMISSIONS_VERIFIED=true` **only after** repeating the permission test for that environment. Confirm a real signed-in user can use group chat/DM and that removal, suspension or block revokes access promptly, including during provider outages.
3. Provide us: app ID/key (public identifier), region, confirmation of where its secret is stored, the role-permissions review outcome, and staging fixture users. Do not provide a secret value in chat. We can run the existing `test:messaging:stream` against the **approved nonproduction app** and finish cross-provider tests.

### 6. Prepare GitHub, Render, DNS and staging before production

1. Own a [GitHub](https://github.com/) repository/organization with MFA and a backup maintainer. Decide whether it is private, who may merge to `master`, who can manage Actions secrets and whether production requires manual approval. Avoid connecting an unreviewed branch or enabling automatic production deployment before staging gates pass. Give us repo location and the desired protected-branch/deploy approvals; we can set up CI when Phase 8 reaches Task 162.
2. Create/own a [Render](https://dashboard.render.com/) workspace; set billing/budget alerts, connect only the intended GitHub repo, and prepare **separate staging and production** static client and NestJS web services. Render's [web-service guide](https://render.com/docs/deploy-node-express-app) and [static-site guide](https://render.com/docs/deploy-create-react-app) show the service creation flow; use Wasl's actual pnpm workspace build/start commands from `package.json` and `server/package.json`, **not** those guides' sample `yarn`/Express/CRA commands. Ask us to supply/review exact root directory, build command, publish directory and Node 24 settings before clicking Deploy; do not guess monorepo path semantics. The SPA needs a `/*` → `/index.html` rewrite for client routes.
3. Decide routing **before** exposing the site: Wasl defaults to same-origin `/api` (`client/.env.example`), but Vite's dev/preview proxy is **not** present on a deployed Render static site. Either arrange an explicit edge/proxy `/api` → NestJS rule with safeguards, **or** build with `VITE_API_URL` pointing at the approved API origin and verify CORS, HTTPS and correct `/api/v1` paths. We will select and implement/test the option with you. `VITE_` variables are shipped to every browser; only URL/publishable key belong there.
4. Prepare secure Render server variables per environment: `NODE_ENV`, `CLIENT_ORIGIN`, `DATABASE_URL`, `SUPABASE_URL`, `SUPABASE_PUBLISHABLE_KEY`, server-only `SUPABASE_SECRET_KEY` or `SUPABASE_SERVICE_ROLE_KEY` if required, `PLATFORM_ADMIN_USER_IDS`, `RESEND_API_KEY`, `RESEND_FROM_EMAIL`, `STREAM_API_KEY`, `STREAM_API_SECRET`, `STREAM_PERMISSIONS_VERIFIED`, and platform-provided `PORT`. Prepare static-site build values `VITE_API_URL` (if cross-origin), `VITE_SUPABASE_URL`, `VITE_SUPABASE_PUBLISHABLE_KEY`. **Use values from the matching environment only**; `server/.env.example` and `client/.env.example` are templates, not secrets. If a variable is not required for a service, confirm that with us before removing it.
5. Ask us to prepare staging config, then set DNS for the agreed hosts using **the records Render supplies**, enable HTTPS and test nested-route refresh, API reachability, signup/reset, Storage, Stream, mail, notification worker and `/api/v1/health`. Note: current `/api/v1/health` returns a timestamp, not a proven database/provider readiness result; health/readiness and monitoring need explicit acceptance. Grant us appropriate scoped, time-limited access if you want us to operate dashboards; otherwise perform the clicks and report redacted results. Do not send a broad account password.
6. Provide us: Render workspace/service IDs or dashboard links, chosen Git branch and deploy policy, staging URLs/DNS status, environment-variable **names and presence only**, build/log links with credentials redacted, and who can change DNS/approve production deploy. Do not deploy production until staging signs off and Phase 7 blockers are closed.

### 7. Agree on measurable gates, backup ownership and operating response

1. Write down the **numeric** pass/fail targets for Phase 8: mobile Lighthouse and Core Web Vitals (including route/device/network conditions and sample size), API latency/error rate, expected concurrent joins/registrations, waitlist/notification lag, email delivery latency, recovery time (RTO), acceptable data loss (RPO), and the agreed WCAG conformance baseline. The guide references SRS gates but the repository does **not** contain numeric ones. Do not present illustrative targets as an approved SRS.
2. Choose the target Supabase plan/backup retention (and PITR if required), plus an independent Storage-object backup/retention approach. Authorize a restore rehearsal on **an isolated nonproduction target** and a non-destructive migration/rollback rehearsal; never restore over production as a test. Assign the person who can initiate recovery and the one who confirms correctness.
3. Set up the operational owners and alert destinations for Render service failures, Supabase health/backups/DB capacity, Stream membership reconciliation, Resend bounces/queue backlog, security abuse/rate limits and privacy requests. Agree on response hours, escalation, maintenance notification, incident communication and emergency secret rotation. Check vendor dashboard status/history rather than assume the previous local test is still valid.
4. Provide us: a short approved gates table, plan/backup details, rehearsal authorization and target, incident/support contacts, monitoring destination and person accountable for go/no-go. We will run the actual measurements and record evidence/exceptions for your decision.

### 8. Provide first-launch content and accountable moderation

1. Decide which real Communities, Events, Organizations and public profiles should be visible on day one, which users own them, and who is authorized to publish on their behalf. Provide approved Arabic/English names, descriptions, categories, cities, dates, admission/capacity policies and real contact/host details. Check accuracy, ownership of images/logos, permission to publish personal data and copyright/licensing. The development reference seed only provides cities/categories; it is not real launch content. Do not copy development test accounts or fixtures into production.
2. Choose the people responsible for reviewing reports, organization verification requests and support/privacy mail; give them access via their own named accounts, not a shared login. Agree on operating hours, escalation for urgent safety/legal reports, user appeal path and a plan when the only moderator is unavailable. Only give platform-admin rights to the named people who need them and have AAL2 enabled.
3. Decide whether invitations to initial organizers and launch partners are needed; have them consent to the approved terms before opening general signups. Provide us a nonsecret content inventory and who will enter/review it in staging and production. We can provide import/check procedures and verify public listings and permissions, but you must approve the real words, rights and owners.

### 9. Stage, sign off and launch

1. After we show passing staging results, personally try the staging site (mobile and desktop, English and Arabic): public discover/details, signup/signin/reset, join and waitlist, create/manage community/event, organization invitation/verification, Stream chat and DM, report/moderation, email notification, file upload, and admin with real AAL2. Use synthetic data; do not involve real users before policies and support are ready. Tell us exactly what failed with URL, expected/actual result and redacted screenshot; no session cookies or tokens.
2. Obtain final counsel approval for displayed texts and process, technical approval of security/restore/results, support readiness and your own written **go/no-go** decision, including any approved exceptions. Close all high/critical defects and stop launch if a required gate is missing. No single agent can provide the owner's sign-off.
3. Approve production project/services, backup state and migration window; have us verify the production target before migrating, deploy a reviewed release and run only non-destructive smoke tests. Have the rollback operator ready. Monitor errors, email queue, provider sync, signup and latency; record the deployed commit, time and decisions. Roll back or pause signups if a launch gate fails.
